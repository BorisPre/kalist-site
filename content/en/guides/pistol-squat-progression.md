The **pistol squat** is a squat on one leg with the other leg held straight in front of you. It’s the benchmark leg skill in calisthenics: strength, balance and mobility in one movement, with no equipment at all.

If you can’t get down or back up, it’s usually not just a lack of strength: ankle mobility, balance and control at the bottom matter just as much. This **pistol squat progression** from the "Pistol squat" path in KALIST trains all three in six steps.

## Before you start

Test your **ankle mobility**: with your foot flat about 10 cm from a wall, can you touch the wall with your knee without lifting your heel? If not, your heel will lift at the bottom of the pistol. Work on your ankles in parallel, and use a support to help you through the progression.

## The 6-step progression

::: step squat
Feet shoulder-width apart, knees tracking over your toes, squat as low as your mobility allows with heels down. A deep, controlled squat is the base of everything.
:::

::: step lunge
Step back, bend both knees, then drive through your front foot to return. The reverse lunge introduces single-leg work with a safety net.
:::

::: step split-squat
Feet fixed in a lunge stance, lower straight down and rise through your front foot. More control than the lunge, and a great single-leg strength builder.
:::

::: step cossack
Feet very wide, shift your weight onto one leg while keeping the other straight out to the side. The cossack squat builds hip and ankle mobility in a deep position.
:::

::: step assisted-pistol
Hold a fixed support (a door frame, bar or post) and lower on one leg with the other straight in front. Use your hands as much as you need, then reduce the help session after session.
:::

::: step pistol
Unassisted: arms forward for balance, lower under control on one foot, then stand back up without bouncing. Go back to the assisted version if your heel lifts or your knee caves in.
:::

## How to train

| Block | Exercise | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Ankle and hip mobility, squats | 5 minutes |
| Strength | Current step, each leg | 3 sets |
| Control | Slow single-leg box squats to a chair | 3 sets of 5 per leg |
| Mobility | Deep squat hold | 2 × 30 seconds |

**Box squats to a chair** are very effective: sit down slowly on one leg, then stand up with both. Lower the box over the weeks.

## Common mistakes

- **Heel lifting.** A sign of stiff ankles: work on mobility and keep using a support.
- **Knee caving in.** Keep your knee in line with your toes, even at the bottom.
- **Dropping into the bottom.** Control the descent: the way up starts from a stable bottom position.
- **Training one side only.** Always train both legs, starting with the weaker one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do I fall backwards in a pistol squat?

Usually because of limited ankle mobility or an upright torso. Reach your arms forward, lean your torso slightly and practise with a support or a small weight held in front of you.

### Are pistol squats bad for your knees?

Done progressively, with your knee in line and no pain, they’re an excellent exercise. If your knee hurts, go back to split squats and get professional advice.

### How long does it take to do a pistol squat?

With good mobility, a few weeks of regular practice is often enough. If your ankles limit you, it takes longer while you gain range.
