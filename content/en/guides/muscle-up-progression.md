The **muscle-up** often marks the move from beginner to experienced in calisthenics: a pull-up that doesn’t stop at the chin but carries you over the bar into a straight-arm support. A good **muscle-up progression** is not just more pull-ups: you need to pull higher and faster, then know how to push once you’re above the bar.

This guide follows the six steps of the "Muscle-up" path in KALIST. It aims for a **strict** muscle-up with no big swing: harder, but safer for your shoulders and more solid in the long run.

## Prerequisites

The muscle-up combines two movements you should already own separately:

- **Pulling**: clean strict pull-ups, with a common benchmark of 8 to 10 in a row before you work on the skill.
- **Pushing**: solid dips, about 10 on parallel bars, then straight bar dips.

If you’re not there yet, start with the [first pull-up guide](/en/guides/first-pull-up/). A solid base gets you to the muscle-up much faster than endless failed attempts.

::: note
The muscle-up puts a lot of load on the shoulders and elbows, especially during the transition. Warm up properly, keep sets short and stop as soon as your technique breaks down.
:::

## The 6-step progression

::: step active-hang
Every muscle-up starts from an active hang: shoulders down, shoulder blades engaged, arms straight. A solid active hang protects your shoulders and gives you a stable base to pull hard.
:::

::: step pullup
Build strict pull-up volume. For the muscle-up, aim for a high pull: your chest travels towards the bar rather than your chin barely clearing it. The higher you pull, the easier the transition.
:::

::: step dip
On parallel bars, lower under control to a comfortable depth, shoulders down, then press up without bouncing. Dips build the pushing strength that finishes the muscle-up.
:::

::: step explosive-pullup
The explosive pull-up is the key to the muscle-up. Pull as fast as you can to bring your chest, then your upper stomach, to the bar. Keep sets short: once the height drops, the set is over. Film yourself to check how high you get.
:::

::: step straight-dip
Straight bar dips copy the end of the muscle-up: supported above the bar, hands in front of you, torso leaning slightly forward. This is where many people get stuck on their first attempts. Train them until they feel easy.
:::

::: step muscle-up
Put it together: an explosive pull to your lower chest, wrists and elbows rolling over the bar as you lean forward, then a straight bar dip to locked arms. Aim for one clean rep first, then build towards 3 × 3.
:::

## Training the transition

The transition, the moment your elbows pass over the bar, is the most common sticking point. Two exercises from the library help:

- the [low bar transition](/en/exercises/#low-bar-transition): feet on the floor, you rehearse getting over the bar without carrying your full body weight;
- the [banded muscle-up](/en/exercises/#banded-muscle-up): the band takes off just enough load to practise the whole movement.

Aim for a **symmetrical** transition, with both elbows passing over together. Getting over one arm at a time (the "chicken wing") loads one side and quickly becomes a bad habit.

## How to train

Two muscle-up focused sessions a week are enough, alongside other calisthenics sessions if you like. Do the explosive work early in the session, while you are fresh.

| Block | Exercise | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Shoulder mobility, active hang, a few easy pull-ups | 8 to 10 minutes |
| Power | Explosive pull-ups | 4 to 5 sets of 2 to 4, full rest |
| Technique | Low bar transition or assisted muscle-up | 3 to 4 short sets |
| Strength | Straight bar dips | 3 sets |

## Common mistakes

- **Pulling too low.** If the bar stops at your chin, the transition is almost impossible. Build height with explosive pull-ups.
- **Keeping the bar away from you.** The muscle-up travels close to the bar: pull towards your hips, not just upwards.
- **A big swing to make up for strength.** A small kip may help at first, but a large one hides a lack of strength and wears out your shoulders.
- **Attempting it tired.** End-of-session attempts are slow and sloppy. Try muscle-ups while you’re fresh.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many pull-ups do you need for a muscle-up?

There is no official number, but a common benchmark is 8 to 10 strict pull-ups and around 10 dips. Even more important: being able to pull your chest to the bar with an explosive pull-up.

### How long does it take to learn the muscle-up?

With a good base of pull-ups and dips, many people get there within a few weeks to a few months of regular practice. Starting from zero takes longer, because you need to build that base first.

### Bar or rings first?

On a bar, the bar doesn’t move but you have to get around it. On rings, the false grip and stability change everything. Start on the bar, then move to the [ring muscle-up](/en/exercises/#ring-muscleup) if you like.
