The **front lever** is one of the most impressive calisthenics skills: hanging from the bar, body straight and horizontal, facing up. It is a pure strength skill that needs a powerful back, a strong core and shoulders that stay engaged with straight arms.

The good news is that the **front lever progression** is very well mapped out. You shorten the lever by tucking your legs, then lengthen it little by little. This guide follows the six steps of the "Front lever" path in KALIST.

## Prerequisites

- Solid **strict pull-ups**: around 8 to 10 reps is a good benchmark.
- A **hollow body** held for at least 30 seconds without your lower back lifting.
- Shoulders that are pain-free when hanging.

The front lever is a straight-arm skill: tendons and connective tissue adapt more slowly than muscles. Progress patiently, even when your muscles feel ready.

## The 6-step progression

::: step hollow
Lying on your back with your lower back pressed into the floor, arms and legs straight and slightly lifted. The hollow body is the exact shape of the front lever, on the floor. If it’s too hard, bend your knees or bring your arms to your sides.
:::

::: step pullup
Pull-ups build the pulling strength the front lever needs. Add slow, controlled reps, and straight-arm pulldowns if you have rings or a band.
:::

::: step tuck-front
Knees to chest, back horizontal, arms straight. Push the bar towards your hips while keeping your shoulders down. Your back should be flat or slightly rounded, with your hips level with your shoulders rather than hanging below the bar. This is your first real front lever position.
:::

::: step advanced-front
From the tuck, move your knees away from your chest until your back is flat, hips at shoulder height, knees still bent. That small change makes the position much heavier. Keep holds short and clean rather than long and sagging.
:::

::: step one-leg-front
One leg straight, the other tucked. Switch sides each set. It’s an excellent bridge to the full front lever, letting you fine-tune the difficulty.
:::

::: step front-lever
Body fully straight and horizontal from shoulders to feet. A clean hold of a few seconds is already a great achievement. Go back to an easier variation as soon as your hips drop or your arms bend.
:::

## How to train

The front lever responds to **repeated short holds** rather than one long hold. Two to three sessions a week are enough.

| Block | Exercise | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Active hang, hollow body, shoulder rotations | 8 minutes |
| Holds | Current step | 5 to 6 holds of 5 to 10 s, 2 min rest |
| Negatives | Slow lowering from support into a tuck or advanced tuck | 3 sets of 2 to 3 |
| Accessories | Pull-ups, hollow body | 2 to 3 sets |

Move to the next step once you hold the current goal with clean form in two sessions in a row.

## Mistakes that cost months

- **Bending the arms.** The front lever is held with straight arms. Bent elbows shift the work and give a false sense of progress.
- **Letting the hips drop.** Hips below shoulder level make it easier, but it’s no longer a front lever. Go back to the tuck.
- **Shrugging.** Keep your shoulders pulled down, as in an active hang.
- **Skipping steps.** Jumping to the full front lever too early gives one-second holds with no training value.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to get a front lever?

It’s a long-term skill. Depending on your starting point, expect several months for a solid advanced tuck, and often much longer for the full front lever. Consistency matters more than intensity.

### Can I train the front lever on rings?

Yes, and rings are often kinder to the wrists. The position is the same: straight arms, shoulders down, body horizontal.

### Do I need to train abs for the front lever?

Core strength is essential, but your lats carry most of the front lever. The hollow body trains both at once, which makes it the most useful accessory exercise.
