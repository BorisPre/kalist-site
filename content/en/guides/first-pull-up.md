Your **first pull-up** is often the first real goal in calisthenics, and the one that causes the most frustration: you grab the bar, you pull… and nothing happens. The good news is that a pull-up is a skill you can build. You strengthen your grip, learn to engage your shoulders, then teach your back and arms to control your body weight.

This guide follows the "First pull-up" path in KALIST: five steps, each with a clear goal. When you reach the goal with clean technique, you move on to the next one.

## Before you start

You need a solid **pull-up bar**: a well-fitted doorway bar, a wall-mounted bar, a rack or a bar at a street workout park. Rings work too. For negatives, use a sturdy platform (a solid chair, bench or step) to reach the top position.

Warm up your shoulders and wrists for a few minutes before every session: arm circles, rotations and a few short hangs. If your shoulder or elbow hurts, get it checked before loading those joints.

::: note
Check that the bar is secure before every set. A doorway bar must sit on a solid frame and never move when you hang from it.
:::

## The 5-step progression

::: step dead-hang
The dead hang builds grip strength and gets your shoulders used to carrying your body weight. Grip the bar overhand, slightly wider than shoulder width, and lift your feet. Stay relaxed and don’t swing. If 20 seconds is too long, keep your toes on the floor to take some weight off.
:::

::: step active-hang
Same position, but pull your shoulders down, away from your ears, without bending your elbows. That is the very first inch of a pull-up: without this shoulder blade engagement, your arms do all the work. Alternate a few seconds active and passive to feel the difference.
:::

::: step australian-row
Australian rows (inverted rows) are done under a low bar, heels on the floor, body braced like a plank. Pull your chest to the bar with elbows close to your body, then lower slowly. The more upright your body, the easier it gets: start with bent knees, straighten your legs to progress.
:::

::: step negative-pullup
Use your platform to get your chin over the bar, then lower yourself as slowly as you can, ideally over 3 to 5 seconds, until your arms are straight. You are strongest on the way down, so negatives are the best way to train the full range before you can pull up. End the set as soon as the descent turns into a drop.
:::

::: step pullup
The strict pull-up: start from an active hang with straight arms, drive your elbows towards your ribs until your chin clears the bar, without swinging or kicking, then lower under control. One clean rep beats three messy ones. Then build volume: 3 × 2, then 3 × 4, up to the goal of 3 × 6.
:::

## How to train

Train pulling **two to three times a week**, with at least one rest day between sessions. Your muscles and tendons need to recover, and recovery is when you get stronger.

| Block | Exercise | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Shoulder circles, dead hang | 5 minutes |
| Strength | Current step (for example negatives) | 3 sets, 2 min rest |
| Volume | Previous step (for example Australian rows) | 3 sets |
| Core | Hollow body or plank | 2 to 3 sets |

Stay on a step until you hit its goal with clean form in **two sessions in a row**. That is the rule KALIST uses to raise your target.

## Common mistakes that slow you down

- **Pulling with your arms only.** Without shoulder blade engagement, your biceps tire quickly and your shoulders creep up to your ears. Go back to active hangs.
- **Swinging.** Kipping makes it feel like you’re getting higher but doesn’t build strict strength. Keep your legs together and your core tight.
- **Cutting the range.** Half a pull-up is half a pull-up. Return to straight arms on every rep.
- **Training every day.** More is not better: without recovery your strength stalls and your elbows may start to ache.
- **Skipping negatives.** Many beginners move to full pull-ups too soon. Slow, clean negatives often make the difference.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to do your first pull-up?

It depends on your starting point, your body weight and your consistency. Training two to three times a week, many beginners get there within a few weeks to a few months. If you can do 3 × 5 slow negatives of 5 seconds, you are usually close.

### Pull-ups or chin-ups first?

Chin-ups (palms facing you) involve more biceps and often feel a little easier. Use them for variety, but keep the overhand pull-up as your main goal: it prepares you for the muscle-up and the front lever.

### Can I use resistance bands?

Yes. A band under your feet or knees helps you train the full range. Combine it with unassisted negatives: bands help most at the bottom, where you need it least.

### I don’t have a low bar for Australian rows. What can I do?

Rings or suspension straps set low work perfectly. Otherwise, spend more time on active hangs and negatives.
