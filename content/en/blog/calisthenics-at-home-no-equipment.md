No gym, no bar, no problem: calisthenics works very well **at home with no equipment**. A bit of floor, a wall and some consistency are enough to train your whole body and progress towards harder exercises.

Here are 12 bodyweight exercises grouped by area, followed by a 20-minute circuit that combines them. All of them are in the KALIST library with their animation and technique cues.

## Upper body: pushing

::: moves knee-pushup, pushup, pike-pushup
:::

1. **Knee push-ups**: the entry point. Keep a straight line from knees to shoulders.
2. **Push-ups**: body braced, elbows at roughly 30 to 60° from your torso, chest to the floor or close to it.
3. **Pike push-ups**: hips high, they shift the work to your shoulders and prepare you for the [handstand](/en/guides/handstand-progression/).

## Legs and glutes

::: moves squat, lunge, split-squat
:::

4. **Bodyweight squats**: heels down, knees tracking over your toes.
5. **Reverse lunges**: gentler on the knees than forward lunges.
6. **Split squats**: feet fixed, straight down and up, great for single-leg strength.

::: moves glute-bridge, calf-raise
:::

7. **Glute bridges**: lying on your back, lift your hips by squeezing your glutes.
8. **Calf raises**: on a step for more range, holding on for balance.

## Core

::: moves plank, side-plank, hollow, dead-bug
:::

9. **Front plank**: shoulders over elbows, lower back not sagging.
10. **Side plank**: hips high, body in line.
11. **Hollow body**: the base position of every skill, lower back pressed into the floor.
12. **Dead bug**: opposite arm and leg extend slowly, lower back stays down.

## The 20-minute circuit

Go through the exercises without stopping, then rest 2 minutes at the end of each round. Do 3 rounds.

| Exercise | Time or reps |
|---|---|
| Push-ups (or knee push-ups) | 8 to 12 |
| Squats | 15 |
| Pike push-ups | 6 to 8 |
| Reverse lunges | 8 per leg |
| Glute bridges | 12 |
| Front plank | 30 s |
| Hollow body | 20 s |

Three sessions a week, with a rest day between them, is enough to make clear progress within a few weeks.

## What about pulling?

That’s the real limit of training without equipment: it’s hard to train your back with nothing to pull on. The simplest fix is a **doorway pull-up bar**: cheap and quick to install. It unlocks hangs, [negative pull-ups](/en/guides/first-pull-up/) and eventually pull-ups.

Until then, strengthen your upper back with planks and hollow holds, and keep your shoulder blades active in your push-ups.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you build muscle with calisthenics at home?

Yes, especially as a beginner, as long as you make the exercises progressively harder: a harder variation, a slower tempo or more reps. Sleep and nutrition do the rest.

### How long should a session last?

Twenty to forty minutes is enough. Consistency beats duration.

### Should I train every day?

No: three sessions a week is an excellent rhythm to start. On rest days, walking and gentle mobility help you recover.
