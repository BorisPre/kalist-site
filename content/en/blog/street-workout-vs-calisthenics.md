**Street workout** or **calisthenics**? Both terms come up constantly in bodyweight training, sometimes as synonyms, sometimes as two different things. Here’s what they share, what sets them apart, and how to choose your way in.

## Calisthenics: training with your body weight

The word **calisthenics** comes from the Greek *kállos*, beauty, and *sthénos*, strength. It means training that uses your own body weight as resistance: push-ups, pull-ups, dips, squats and core work, then more advanced skills.

It’s a broad term. People do calisthenics at home, in the gym or at the park, for strength, fitness or skills.

## Street workout: calisthenics in the street

**Street workout** grew out of training in public spaces: park bars, outdoor fitness areas and urban structures. It took off in the 2000s through videos shared online, with a strong group and showcase culture.

Street workout has its own disciplines:

- **freestyle**: dynamic moves on the bar (spins, releases, combinations);
- **static holds**: skills such as the [front lever](/en/guides/front-lever-progression/), the [planche](/en/guides/planche-progression/) or the human flag;
- **strength and endurance**: rep challenges and sometimes weighted work.

Competitions exist in many countries, in both freestyle and strength.

## So what’s the difference?

| | Calisthenics | Street workout |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Bodyweight training | Calisthenics practised outdoors, with its own culture |
| Where | Anywhere: home, gym, park | Mostly parks and urban structures |
| Goal | Strength, fitness, skills | Skills, freestyle, performance, community |
| Equipment | From nothing to bars, rings, parallettes | Park bars, parallel bars |

In practice, **street workout is one way of doing calisthenics**. The basic exercises are the same; the setting, the culture and some disciplines (like freestyle) differ.

## How to get started

Whatever you call it, the foundations are the same:

::: moves pushup, pullup, dip
:::

1. **Build the basics**: push-ups, pull-ups, dips, squats and core. The [beginner program](/en/blog/beginner-calisthenics-program/) organises them into three sessions a week.
2. **Pick a first skill**: your [first pull-up](/en/guides/first-pull-up/), the [L-sit](/en/guides/l-sit-progression/) or the [handstand](/en/guides/handstand-progression/) are all beginner-friendly.
3. **Go to the park** if you have one nearby: the atmosphere and advice from experienced athletes often save a lot of time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is street workout suitable for beginners?

Yes. Street workout parks welcome every level, and low bars let you start with Australian rows or assisted dips.

### Do I need a gym for calisthenics?

No. Home is enough to start, and a park or a pull-up bar lets you go further. See [12 exercises to do at home with no equipment](/en/blog/calisthenics-at-home-no-equipment/).

### Calisthenics or weight training?

Both build strength. Calisthenics adds body control, balance and skills to learn; weight training lets you load each muscle precisely. Many people combine the two.
