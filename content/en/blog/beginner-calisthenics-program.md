Want to start calisthenics but not sure where to begin? This **beginner calisthenics program** fits into two workouts, A and B, alternated three times a week. It trains your whole body, needs very little equipment and gives you clear benchmarks to progress week after week.

It follows the same principles as the beginner programs in KALIST: few exercises, clean technique, and a target that only goes up when you’re ready.

## How the program works

- **Full body every session.** Push, pull, legs and core: as a beginner, training each area two to three times a week gives the best results.
- **Rep ranges.** Instead of a fixed number you aim for, say, 8 to 12 reps. When you hit the top of the range on every set, two sessions in a row, move to a harder variation.
- **A rest day between sessions.** Monday, Wednesday and Friday, for example.
- **Technique first.** A rep that loses its alignment doesn’t count.

## Equipment

The floor is enough for most exercises. A **pull-up bar** (doorway or park bar) is strongly recommended for pulling. A low bar, rings or straps let you do Australian rows. Two sturdy chairs can replace parallel bars for assisted dips.

## Warm-up (5 to 8 minutes)

Arm circles, wrist rotations, 10 slow squats, 10 easy incline push-ups and a 20-second hang from the bar. The goal is to reach your first set with warm joints, not tired muscles.

## Workout A

::: moves pushup, australian-row, squat
:::

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Push-ups (or incline push-ups) | 3 × 8 to 12 | 90 s |
| Australian rows | 3 × 8 to 12 | 90 s |
| Bodyweight squats | 3 × 12 to 15 | 60 s |
| Front plank | 3 × 20 to 30 s | 45 s |
| Hollow body | 2 × 15 to 20 s | 45 s |

## Workout B

::: moves negative-pullup, assisted-dip, lunge
:::

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Negative pull-ups (or active hangs) | 3 × 3 to 5 | 2 min |
| Assisted dips | 3 × 6 to 10 | 90 s |
| Reverse lunges | 3 × 8 to 10 per leg | 60 s |
| Glute bridges | 3 × 12 to 15 | 60 s |
| Side plank | 2 × 20 s per side | 45 s |

## Your week

| Week | Monday | Wednesday | Friday |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | A | B | A |
| Week 2 | B | A | B |

Follow this rhythm for **six weeks**, then take stock: which exercises have become easy? Move to the next variation (incline push-ups to full push-ups, negatives to pull-ups) and start a new cycle.

## Scaling each exercise

Every exercise has an easier and a harder version:

- **Push-ups**: wall → incline → knee → full → diamond.
- **Pulling**: Australian rows with bent knees → straight legs → negatives → pull-ups.
- **Legs**: squat → reverse lunge → split squat → Bulgarian split squat → pistol squat.
- **Core**: plank → hollow body → tuck L-sit.

You’ll find all of these, animated, in the [exercise library](/en/exercises/).

::: note
Sharp joint pain is not muscle soreness: stop the exercise and go back to an easier variation. If in doubt, ask a health professional.
:::

## Frequently asked questions

### How long before I see results from calisthenics?

Rep numbers often improve within two to three weeks as your body learns to coordinate the movements. Visible physical changes usually take several weeks of consistent training, along with good food and sleep.

### Can I do this program every day?

It’s not recommended: muscles and tendons get stronger during recovery. On rest days, walk, stretch or practise balance gently.

### I don’t have a pull-up bar. What should I do?

Replace pulling with Australian rows on rings or straps, or get a doorway pull-up bar: it’s the single most useful piece of calisthenics equipment. Without pulling, the program is unbalanced.

### When should I start training skills like the muscle-up?

Once your basics are solid: around 8 to 10 pull-ups and 10 dips for the muscle-up, for example. The [progression guides](/en/guides/) list the prerequisites for each skill.
