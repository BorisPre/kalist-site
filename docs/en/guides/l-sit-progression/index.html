L-sit progression for beginners: from plank to V-sit

Learn the L-sit: plank, compression, tuck L-sit, one-leg L-sit, full L-sit and V-sit. Hold targets, equipment and the mistakes to avoid.

6 steps3 min readUpdated 22 November 2026

The L-sit is a core skill held on your hands, legs straight out in front at hip height: your body forms an "L". It looks simple, but it combines three often neglected qualities: shoulder pushing strength, hip flexor strength and abdominal compression.

It’s also a great skill for beginners, because the L-sit progression is clear and needs very little equipment. Here are the six steps of the "L-sit" path in KALIST.

Equipment

Parallettes are ideal: they leave room for your legs and spare your wrists. Two sturdy chairs or the parallel bars at a park also work. A floor L-sit is possible but harder, because you have very little room to lift your hips.

The 6-step progression

Step 1

Forearm plank

  • Beginner
  • No equipment
  • Goal : 3 × 30 s

The front plank builds the base: shoulders over your hands or elbows, body in line, steady breathing. End the hold before your lower back starts to sag.

Muscles : Core, Shoulders

Step 2

Seated compression lift

  • Intermediate
  • No equipment
  • Goal : 3 × 10 reps

Sit with straight legs and hands beside your thighs, then briefly lift your heels without leaning back. This is what most beginners are missing: compression teaches you to lift your legs from the hips.

Muscles : Core, Hip flexors, Triceps

Step 3

Tuck L-sit

  • Beginner
  • Parallel bars
  • Goal : 3 × 15 s

On parallettes with straight arms and shoulders down, bring your knees to your chest and lift your feet. Push the bars away as if you were trying to grow taller. Put your feet down before your shoulders lose position.

Muscles : Core, Hip flexors, Triceps

Step 4

One-leg L-sit

  • Intermediate
  • Parallel bars
  • Goal : 3 × 12 s

One knee tucked, the other leg straight out in front. Hold the same time on each side. This step links the tuck to the full L-sit.

Muscles : Core, Hip flexors, Triceps

Step 5

L-sit

  • Intermediate
  • Parallel bars
  • Goal : 3 × 15 s

Both legs straight at hip height, toes pointed. Arms stay locked and shoulders away from your ears. If your legs drop, bend your knees slightly rather than losing height.

Muscles : Core, Hip flexors, Triceps

Step 6

V-sit

  • Advanced
  • Parallel bars
  • Goal : 3 × 8 s

The V-sit raises your legs above horizontal and needs serious compression and flexibility. It’s an advanced goal to work on once your L-sit is solid.

Muscles : Core, Hip flexors, Triceps

How to train

Three times a week, at the end of a session or as a short dedicated one:

BlockExerciseVolume
Warm-upWrists, shoulders, compression5 minutes
HoldsCurrent step5 holds, 90 s rest
CompressionSeated leg lifts3 sets of 8 to 10
FlexibilityHamstring stretch2 to 3 minutes

Hamstring flexibility makes a big difference: straight legs in front of you need less effort when the backs of your thighs aren’t pulling against you.

Common mistakes

  • Shrugged shoulders. Push the bars away with your shoulders down: that’s half the L-sit.
  • Bent arms. Keep your elbows locked.
  • Rounded back and low hips. Keep your chest open and your hips level with your hands.
  • Skipping compression. Without it, your legs won’t come up no matter how strong your arms are.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to hold an L-sit?

A 15-second tuck L-sit often comes within a few weeks. A full 10 to 15-second L-sit usually takes several weeks to a few months, depending on your flexibility and core strength.

Can you do an L-sit on the floor?

Yes, but it’s harder: you need long arms relative to your torso or very good compression to lift your hips and heels. Start on parallettes or two sturdy chairs.

Does the L-sit work your abs?

Yes, along with your hip flexors, triceps and shoulders. It’s a complete core exercise.
