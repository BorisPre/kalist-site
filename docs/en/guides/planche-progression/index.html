Planche progression: from tuck planche to full planche

Learn the calisthenics planche: wrist preparation, planche lean, tuck planche, advanced tuck, straddle and full planche. Training tips and common mistakes.

7 steps3 min readUpdated 22 November 2026

The planche is often seen as the king of calisthenics skills: body horizontal, supported on your hands with straight arms, feet off the floor. It demands very strong shoulders, prepared wrists and a lot of patience.

This guide lays out the planche progression as it is organised in KALIST: seven steps, from wrist preparation to the full planche. The first ones suit beginners; the last ones are a long-term project.

Before you start

The planche is held with straight arms. That puts a lot of load on the tendons of your elbows, wrists and shoulders, which adapt more slowly than muscles. That is the number one reason to progress slowly.

Use parallettes if you can: they spare your wrists with a neutral grip. The floor works for the early steps too.

Sharp pain in the wrist or elbow means stop. Reduce the lean, go back a step and give your joints time to adapt.

The 7-step progression

Step 1

Wrist preparation

  • Beginner
  • No equipment
  • Goal : 3 × 30 s

On all fours with palms flat, gently rock your weight forwards and back, then side to side. Add a few rotations with your fingers pointing towards your knees. A few minutes before each planche session is enough.

Muscles : Forearms

Step 2

Scapular push-up

  • Beginner
  • No equipment
  • Goal : 3 × 12 reps

In a push-up position with straight arms, let your chest sink between your shoulder blades, then push the floor away to spread them as far as possible. That spread (protraction) is exactly the shoulder position of the planche.

Muscles : Chest, Triceps, Shoulders

Step 3

Planche lean

  • Intermediate
  • No equipment
  • Goal : 3 × 15 s

In a push-up position with feet on the floor, lean your shoulders forward past your hands, arms straight and shoulder blades spread. The further you lean, the harder it gets. The planche lean is the exercise you’ll keep at every stage.

Muscles : Shoulders, Core, Chest

Step 4

Tuck planche

  • Advanced
  • Parallel bars
  • Goal : 3 × 8 s

On parallettes, lean forward with your knees tucked to your chest and lift your feet. Your back is rounded and your arms straight. At first, hold a few seconds and put your feet down before your shoulders lose their position.

Muscles : Shoulders, Core, Chest

Step 5

Advanced tuck planche

  • Advanced
  • Parallel bars
  • Goal : 3 × 8 s

Open your hips to bring your back level, knees still bent. This is a long stage: take the time to own it before straightening your legs.

Muscles : Shoulders, Core, Chest

Step 6

Straddle planche

  • Advanced
  • Parallel bars
  • Goal : 3 × 5 s

Legs straight and wide apart: the straddle shortens the lever compared with legs together. Decent hip mobility helps keep your legs up.

Muscles : Shoulders, Core, Chest

Step 7

Full planche

  • Advanced
  • Parallel bars
  • Goal : 3 × 5 s

Legs straight and together, body fully horizontal. For most people it’s the result of several years of work. Every second counts.

Muscles : Shoulders, Core, Chest

How to train

BlockExerciseVolume
PreparationWrists, scapular push-ups5 to 8 minutes
HoldsCurrent step5 to 8 short holds of 5 to 10 s
LeanPlanche lean3 sets of 15 to 20 s
StrengthPseudo planche push-ups, dips3 sets

Two to three planche sessions a week are plenty. Pseudo planche push-ups, hands turned towards your hips and shoulders forward, are an excellent accessory.

Common mistakes

  • Bending the elbows. A bent-arm planche is a different exercise. Keep your elbows locked.
  • Collapsing the shoulders. Without protraction, your shoulders sink and the position falls apart. Keep pushing the floor away.
  • Neglecting your wrists. Two minutes of preparation saves a lot of pain.
  • Straightening the legs too soon. The advanced tuck is where you’ll spend the longest. Don’t skip it.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to learn the planche?

The full planche is one of the longest skills to achieve and often takes several years. The tuck planche comes much sooner with regular practice.

Can I learn the planche without parallettes?

Yes, on the floor. Parallettes or push-up handles make the position more comfortable for your wrists and help your feet clear the floor.

Planche or front lever first?

They complement each other: the planche is a pushing skill, the front lever a pulling one. Many people train both on different days.
