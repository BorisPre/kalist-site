Calisthenics at home with no equipment: 12 exercises to start

12 calisthenics exercises you can do at home with no equipment, for upper body, legs and core, plus a 20-minute beginner circuit.

3 min readUpdated 21 November 2026

No gym, no bar, no problem: calisthenics works very well at home with no equipment. A bit of floor, a wall and some consistency are enough to train your whole body and progress towards harder exercises.

Here are 12 bodyweight exercises grouped by area, followed by a 20-minute circuit that combines them. All of them are in the KALIST library with their animation and technique cues.

Upper body: pushing

Knee push-up

Beginner3 × 10 reps

Push-up

Beginner3 × 10 reps

Pike push-up

Intermediate3 × 8 reps
  1. Knee push-ups: the entry point. Keep a straight line from knees to shoulders.
  2. Push-ups: body braced, elbows at roughly 30 to 60° from your torso, chest to the floor or close to it.
  3. Pike push-ups: hips high, they shift the work to your shoulders and prepare you for the handstand.

Legs and glutes

Bodyweight squat

Beginner3 × 15 reps

Reverse lunge

Beginner3 × 10 reps

Split squat

Beginner3 × 10 reps
  1. Bodyweight squats: heels down, knees tracking over your toes.
  2. Reverse lunges: gentler on the knees than forward lunges.
  3. Split squats: feet fixed, straight down and up, great for single-leg strength.

Glute bridge

Beginner3 × 15 reps

Single-leg calf raise

Beginner3 × 15 reps
  1. Glute bridges: lying on your back, lift your hips by squeezing your glutes.
  2. Calf raises: on a step for more range, holding on for balance.

Core

Forearm plank

Beginner3 × 30 s

Side plank

Beginner3 × 20 s

Hollow body hold

Beginner3 × 20 s

Dead bug

Beginner3 × 10 reps
  1. Front plank: shoulders over elbows, lower back not sagging.
  2. Side plank: hips high, body in line.
  3. Hollow body: the base position of every skill, lower back pressed into the floor.
  4. Dead bug: opposite arm and leg extend slowly, lower back stays down.

The 20-minute circuit

Go through the exercises without stopping, then rest 2 minutes at the end of each round. Do 3 rounds.

ExerciseTime or reps
Push-ups (or knee push-ups)8 to 12
Squats15
Pike push-ups6 to 8
Reverse lunges8 per leg
Glute bridges12
Front plank30 s
Hollow body20 s

Three sessions a week, with a rest day between them, is enough to make clear progress within a few weeks.

What about pulling?

That’s the real limit of training without equipment: it’s hard to train your back with nothing to pull on. The simplest fix is a doorway pull-up bar: cheap and quick to install. It unlocks hangs, negative pull-ups and eventually pull-ups.

Until then, strengthen your upper back with planks and hollow holds, and keep your shoulder blades active in your push-ups.

Frequently asked questions

Can you build muscle with calisthenics at home?

Yes, especially as a beginner, as long as you make the exercises progressively harder: a harder variation, a slower tempo or more reps. Sleep and nutrition do the rest.

How long should a session last?

Twenty to forty minutes is enough. Consistency beats duration.

Should I train every day?

No: three sessions a week is an excellent rhythm to start. On rest days, walking and gentle mobility help you recover.
